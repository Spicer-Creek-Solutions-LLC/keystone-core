# Dossier — C04, local operator control substrate

The fourth Stage C task, and the first that lets a human reach the server.

C01 built the envelope, C02 the stores, C03 the authorization a broker enforces.
None of them has an operator. `ADR-0009` decided the one local surface —
`/run/keystone/operator.sock` — and who may cross it. C04 builds that surface and
the authorization behind it, **and no journey across it**.

**It is a substrate task with no journey and no broker.** `ADR-0009` § 4 maps the
charter's seven journeys to `admin`, and C04 wires none of them: `C05` is the
first task with a verb. Nothing here publishes to NATS, and
`internal/cli/boundary_test.go` still refuses a journey verb anywhere in non-test
source.

This dossier is a boundary, not an approval. Landing it authorizes nothing: C04
still needs its own presented plan and explicit approval, and it is a
**behaviour workstream**, so it splits into `C04-A` and `C04-I` with different
agents per [`REBOOT-EXECUTION-PLAN.md`](../project/REBOOT-EXECUTION-PLAN.md)
§ "Required task dossier". Roles alternate from C03: **`C04-A` is Claude's to
write and Codex's to review**, and `C04-I` the reverse.

### 5.1 The `TESTING.md` feature table

### 5.2 The contract's cases

`C04-A` freezes these. The identifiers are its own, since `ADR-0009` enumerates
no case list the way `ADR-0004` §§ 8–9 did.

| Case | Fails when |
|---|---|
| The socket's directory or file has the wrong owner, group or mode | § 1 |
| The server starts with no admin group configured | § 1 — an unstated group is a configuration error |
| A principal outside the group can `stat` the socket or learn the server is running | § 1's traversal rule |
| **A request byte is parsed before the connection is authorized** | § 3, and see `D-C04-1` |
| `SO_PEERCRED` is not read before any request byte is read | § 3 |
| A stale-group session connects and is not refused by the in-band check | § 3 — the case the kernel's mode cannot catch |
| `root` is authorized without § 4 saying so | § 3 |
| The kernel's refusal and the in-band refusal are distinguishable to the caller | § 5 |
| Either refusal path exits other than `10` | § 5 |
| `ENOENT` exits other than `1` | § 5 |
| An in-band denial produces no record | § 6 |
| A kernel refusal produces a record | § 6 — the unrecorded set is named, not accidental |
| A framing byte must be read to decide authorization | § 10 |
| An operator action reaches an agent without traversing the broker | § 11 |

**The two that matter are the ordering pair.** A denial proves the *outcome*; §3
requires an *order*, and the difference is only visible when the parser is
instrumented or the connection is stopped between steps. `D-C04-1` proposes how.

### 5.3 Demonstrated failing, and what these cases cannot detect

Every case is demonstrated against a planted defect before it is trusted, per
`DL-1`, and the standard C03 settled applies: **one mutation per case, planted in
the input that case inspects, with the failure set recorded per mutation**,
because a case that fires on every defect is not discriminating.

Two traps specific to this task:

- **A denial case is satisfied by a server that refuses everyone.** C03-I3 found
  the same shape at the broker: a connection that can do nothing satisfies every
  denial. Each refusal case must exercise an operation the principal *is*
  permitted on the same connection.
- **An ordering case is satisfied by a correct outcome.** Authorizing after
  parsing still denies the right callers; it denies them later. A case that only
  observes the refusal cannot see the defect it exists for.

**What these cases cannot detect.** That `ADR-0009` § 4's single authority is the
right policy; that the configured group contains the right people; that a
principal the kernel refused did not exist — § 6 concedes this and § 12 records
the cost.

## 11. Decisions requiring confirmation at C04 approval

### `D-C04-1` — how an ordering obligation is frozen

`ADR-0009` § 3 is four rows of *before this, that must have completed*. **A test
that connects and is refused proves the outcome and not the order**, and an
implementation that authorizes after parsing passes it.

This is `D-C02-3`'s problem in a new place. There, receipt and start looked
identical at rest and the answer was a harness that stopped the process at a
chosen commit boundary. Here the candidates are:

| Option | What it proves | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A parser that records when it was first entered**, compared against when authorization completed | The order directly, in the production path | A seam in production code that exists for a test — the thing `ADR-0010` § 3 is suspicious of |
| **A connection that sends bytes and then closes before authorization could finish** | That unparsed bytes were not acted on | Timing-dependent, and proves a weaker property |
| **A build-tagged assertion inside the parser** | The order, without a runtime seam | Only in the tagged build, so the shipped path is not the tested one |

**Proposed: the first**, with the seam being an ordering record the substrate
keeps anyway for its audit decision, not an inserted hook. What is worth
confirming is whether that counts as a test-only adapter under `ADR-0010` § 3.

### `D-C04-2` — the register says nothing about C04

**No row lands at C04, and no row cites `ADR-0009`.** The register's `Lands at`
column names C01, C02, C03, C05, C06, C07, C08, C12, C14, C15 and P11 — not C04.

`ADR-0009`'s own invariant-coverage table claims six invariants, and the register
assigns every one elsewhere: `ARCH-COMM-001` to C06, `-002` to P11, `-003` to
C05, `ARCH-OBS-001` to C12, `ARCH-NATS-003` and `ARCH-TEST-002` to C03.

So **`archlint` will check nothing for C04**, and the substrate's correctness
would rest on its own contract alone. That is the *check that cannot fail* shape
applied to traceability: a task whose obligations no register row records.

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Add a row for the socket's own properties**, sourced to `ADR-0009`, landing at C04 | The register gains an invariant it does not have; invariants are `ARCHITECTURE-INVARIANTS.md`'s, which is out of bounds for C04 |
| **Move `ARCH-COMM-001` to C04** | § 11's property is C04's and its evidence is `test/architecture/nats_only_test.go`, which C06 owns. Splitting a row is worse than either half |
| **Credit C04 on an existing row's evidence** without moving `Lands at` | Honest and small, and leaves `archlint` still checking nothing here |
| **Accept that C04 is tracked by its contract alone**, and say so | No document changes; the gap is recorded rather than closed |

**Proposed: the last, with the gap stated in `C04-I`'s evidence.** The register
tracks invariants, and C04 implements a *decision document* rather than an
invariant. But this is the maintainer's: the alternative reading is that
`ADR-0009` § 11 deserves an invariant of its own, and that is a change to an
approved document C04 may not make.

### `D-C04-3` — where the in-band check learns current membership

§ 3 insists the in-band check is not redundant, and the reason is precise:
supplementary group membership is **cached in a running process's credentials**,
so a revoked operator's existing shell still passes the kernel's check on
`connect()`. Only a re-derivation against *current* membership denies them.

**`ADR-0009` does not say where current membership comes from.** The candidates —
`getgrnam` against NSS, a re-read of `/etc/group`, or a configured list — differ
in what they trust and in whether they work when NSS reaches a network directory
that is unreachable.

This matters beyond tidiness: **the check that fails closed on a directory
timeout denies every operator**, and the one that fails open grants a revoked
one. Neither is obviously right, and `ADR-0009` § 3 assumes the check exists
without deciding its source.

**Proposed: raise, do not fix.** C04 implements against NSS as the platform's
answer, states the failure mode it chose, and the deficiency is recorded for a
G task or a P09 amendment. Deciding it under a C04 approval would settle an
authorization question in an implementation task.

### `D-C04-4` — what C04 wires, and what it must not

`ADR-0009` § 4 maps seven journeys and § 5 puts two exit codes in the CLI. **C04
wires no journey verb**, because `internal/cli/boundary_test.go` forbids every one
of those verbs as a literal in non-test source and C05 is the first task with a
verb to wire.

That leaves § 5's CLI mapping with nothing to map. **Proposed: the server's
listener and the in-band decision land at C04; the CLI's `EACCES` → `10` mapping
lands with the first verb at C05**, and `C04-I`'s evidence states § 5 as owed
rather than implying the contract covers it.

The alternative is a verbless command that connects and exits, which invents a
surface the charter does not have in order to test one it does. **C03 showed what
a dossier costs when § 3.2 and § 3.3 disagree**; this states the seam in both.

- **Any journey** — C05 onward. § 4's table is a projection of the charter, and
  C04 implements the authority check it names, not the operations.
- **Emitting audit records** — C12. § 6 decides which denials are recorded; the
  writing is C12's, as `ARCH-OBS-001`'s row says.
- **The identity change and process execution** — C07. `boundary_test.go` names
  `golang.org/x/sys/unix` as *"C04's and C07's"*; C04 takes it for `SO_PEERCRED`
  and nothing else.
- **Widening the socket to make denials observable** — `ADR-0009` § 2 declined
  it, and the reason is that the recorded population would become everyone on
  the host.
- **`RSK-8`'s acceptance.** A compromised operator workstation acting with the
  operator's authority is accepted risk; § 1 keeps it from becoming *granting
  that authority to anyone*, and that is the whole of what C04 changes about it.
