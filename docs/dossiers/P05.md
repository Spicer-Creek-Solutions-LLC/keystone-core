# Dossier — P05, Versioned encrypted protocol ADR

The task that decides what is inside an envelope, who signs it, who can read it,
and what a receiver does with one it does not understand. It is the fifth of RFC
0001's six cumulative security layers, and the first that protects content
rather than access.

This dossier is a boundary, not an approval. Landing it authorizes nothing: P05
still needs its own presented plan and explicit approval. P05 is a planning and
ADR task and may use one pull request; it does not split into `-A` and `-I`.
Program rule 6's separation of implementation from acceptance review is met by
the independent reviewer named in § 8.

**P03 is the dependency that matters most and is easiest to overlook.**
`ADR-0003` § 4 and § 6 record the agent's **signing verification key** and
**encryption public key** at enrollment, durably, against the issued identity.
P05 encrypts a command *to* the second and verifies a result *against* the
first. Those halves are P05's only source of truth for a recipient, and
`ADR-0003` records them because P04's review found they were being established
and then dropped.

## 3. Exact outputs

One ADR, `docs/adr/0005-versioned-encrypted-protocol.md`, containing:

1. **Envelope canonicalization** — the byte-exact form a signature is computed over, and why a receiver can reproduce it
2. **Version negotiation** — how a version is carried, and what a receiver does with one it does not understand
3. **Job and correlation identifiers** — their form, their uniqueness domain, and which layer assigns them
4. **Signatures** — who signs each message class, with which key, over which bytes
5. **Recipient encryption** — who can decrypt each class, using which recorded public half (`ADR-0003` § 6)
6. **Replay bounds** — the replay key and window per class, and their relationship to `Nats-Msg-Id` and to the agent ledger (`ARCH-JOB-003`)
7. **Timestamps and nonces** — what each is for, and what clock assumption each rests on
8. **Headers** — the complete set, each routing-safe. **Never a secret, a token, or plaintext command content** (`ARCH-OBS-001`, `THR-27`)
9. **Payload and output limits** — expressed against `ADR-0002` § 9's broker limits rather than independently
10. **Forward compatibility** — what a receiver must tolerate, and what it must refuse
11. **The coverage a test-vector set must have** — which inputs, fields and boundary cases it must exercise. **No vector value.** C01 computes them
12. **Error codes** — what a sender learns when an envelope is refused, without learning why in a way that assists an attacker
13. **The message classification**, as a table: enrollment, command, cancellation, result, lifecycle event and presence, each by **signer, verifier, encryption recipient, replay key and window, maximum size, durability, retention, headers, and accepted metadata leakage**

Enumerable content is carried as **tables**, so the acceptance cases read
structure rather than prose.

### 5.3 Demonstrated failing, and what these cases cannot detect

Every case is demonstrated failing before acceptance, with the `DL-7`
assertions applied to each planting — the anchor unique, the document changed,
**the mutated document parsed to confirm the defect is present**, and the failure
naming the case. P03's evidence records two plantings that passed the first two
assertions and not the third; P04's records one anchor that was simply wrong.
Both were caught by the assertions rather than by review, which is the argument
for running all four rather than three.

| Case | Cannot detect | Found instead by |
|---|---|---|
| `AC-1` | Whether an attribute's value is **right** — a class with nine plausible wrong answers passes | Review; C01, when an attribute cannot be implemented |
| `AC-2` | Whether a header is routing-safe in **practice**. A field named innocuously can still carry a secret | Review; `TESTING.md`'s canary scan, which plants secrets and fails before upload |
| `AC-3` | Whether the expression **satisfies** the clause, as opposed to restating it | Review; C01 and C14 |
| `AC-4` | Whether signing cancellations actually closes `RSK-11`, or merely appears to | Review; and the invariant amendment that follows, which is where the gap is settled |
| `AC-5` | Whether the stated coverage is **sufficient** — the boundary case nobody thought of is invisible here | C01, when it computes vectors and finds an unspecified case; C14's fuzzing |
| `AC-6` | Whether the negotiation is **safe** — a receiver that tolerates too much is a downgrade path | Review; C14 |
| `AC-7` | Whether a renewal is **justified** or a resolution **correct** | The maintainer, who owns each risk by name |
| `AC-8` | Whether the recorded public half is the **right** one for that recipient | C01 and C03 |
| `AC-9` | Whether the replay window is **long enough**, or short enough | C14's adversarial suite |

`AC-1` and `AC-5` carry the most weight and the least certainty: between them
they assert that every message is classified and every vector case named, and
neither can tell you an answer is correct or a coverage complete.

## 8. Security reviewer

**Codex, named here rather than at approval.**

Program rule 6 separates security-sensitive work from its acceptance review.
P05 decides confidentiality and integrity — the fourth and fifth of RFC 0001's
six cumulative layers — and it is the first layer that protects content from a
party who is *authorized to carry it*. A defect here is invisible to every
broker-side control `ADR-0002` and `ADR-0004` provide.

The remit is the semantic properties in § 5.3: whether a header is routing-safe
in practice, whether a replay window is the right length, and whether version
negotiation leaves a downgrade path.

`DL-8` applies to how P05 acts on every finding. **Follow the steps in the
ledger entry itself, not a summary here.** `G12` is the cautionary case: four
review rounds, because each fix went to the instances reported and the sweep's
pattern covered one of the shape's several vocabularies.

### `D-P05-1` — the ADR is `0005`

control-document task, as `ADR-0002` through `0004` left it.

### `D-P05-2` — `RSK-11` is resolved in the ADR, and the invariant is amended afterwards by RFC

`RSK-11` records that `ARCH-NATS-006` requires **commands and results** to be
signed and never names **cancellations**, so a holder of the command-publisher
credential alone might produce an accepted cancellation.

`ADR-0005` specifies that cancellation envelopes are signed. The invariant says
commands and results are signed; it does not say *only* those, so requiring more
does not contradict it. **That closes the exposure and not the gap** — the
invariant still would not mandate a signed cancellation, and a later ADR could
drop it without violating anything.

The gap is closed afterwards, by an **RFC amending `ARCH-NATS-006` to name
cancellations**, citing `ADR-0005` as the design it ratifies. The order is
deliberate: an amendment first would put a protocol requirement into a normative
invariant before the task that owns protocol decisions had made one.

**`REQUIREMENTS-TRACEABILITY.md` § Maintenance rules compels an RFC only for
"removing or weakening a requirement", and this strengthens one.** An RFC is
therefore permitted rather than required, and is chosen because a change to an
invariant every later ADR is measured against deserves the weight. It is
Generation 2's first RFC and its own task.

`ADR-0005` must say explicitly that it closes the exposure and not the invariant
gap, so a reader between the two tasks is not misled.

### `D-P05-3` — P05 specifies the protocol, it does not implement it

No code, no canonical encoder, no signatures computed, **and no test-vector
values**. `G13` corrected the workstream paragraph that implied otherwise, and
`AC-5` fails the ADR if a vector value appears. A vector written by hand before
an implementation exists is a number the implementation will be tuned to match.

### `D-P05-4` — a defect in `ADR-0002`, `ADR-0003` or `ADR-0004` is raised, not fixed

P05 is the first task depending on three accepted ADRs at once.
