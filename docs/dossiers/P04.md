# Dossier — P04, Subject authorization ADR and executable policy

The task that writes down what every principal may say and what it must be
refused. P02 and P03 each named subjects **by role** and deferred the grammar
here; P04 is where those deferrals are discharged or found unrepresentable.

This dossier is a boundary, not an approval. Landing it authorizes nothing: P04
still needs its own presented plan and explicit approval. P04 is a planning and
ADR task and may use one pull request; it does not split into `-A` and `-I`.
Program rule 6's separation of implementation from acceptance review is met by
the independent reviewer named in § 8.

## 3. Exact outputs

One ADR, `docs/adr/0004-subject-authorization.md`, containing:

1. **The canonical subject grammar** — stated once, normatively, with its token
   structure and what each token may contain
2. **Every subject class the seven charter journeys need**, expressed in that
   grammar: command, cancellation, result, event, presence, enrollment request
   and reply
3. **The principal-by-subject permission matrix** — for each principal of
   `ADR-0002` § 3, its publish and subscribe entries, with the scope each is
   bound to
4. **The data-plane `$JS.API` and reply allowlist** as literal shapes, in the
   two entry shapes `ADR-0002` § 8 fixed: consumer-scoped entries naming one
   exact stream and consumer, principal-scoped inboxes naming neither
5. **Wildcard rules** — no wildcard across agent identifiers (`ARCH-NATS-003`);
   where a trailing wildcard is permitted inside an entry's own prefix, and the
   justification required per entry
6. **The `FilterSubject` rule** — one exact filter per consumer. Any multi-filter
   use must account for its authorization behaviour explicitly, which the
   execution plan requires by name
7. **NATS configuration shape** — the account and user structures C03 generates,
   as a specification rather than a file
8. **JWT claim shapes** per principal, including the enrollment-time bootstrap
   identity of `ADR-0003` § 3
9. **Positive authorization cases** — for each principal, what it may do, named
   so C03 can implement each as a test
10. **Negative authorization cases** — what each principal must be refused,
    covering all five of `ARCH-TEST-002`'s clauses by name
11. **What C03 implements from this**, stated explicitly, so the boundary
    between specification and generation is in the document rather than only in
    the execution plan

Enumerable content is carried as **tables** — the grammar's tokens, the matrix,
the allowlist, the case lists — so the acceptance cases read structure rather
than prose. That is `DL-2`'s countermeasure applied before the fact.

**P04 is where literal subject strings finally appear.** P02's `AC-8` and P03's
`AC-8` both forbade them, because the grammar was not yet decided. That
prohibition ends here, and the inverse obligation begins: every subject the
prior ADRs named by role must now be written out.

### 5.2 Document acceptance cases

| ID | Claim | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| `AC-1` | The grammar is stated once, normatively, and **every subject appearing anywhere in the ADR parses under it** | A subject appears that the stated grammar does not generate, or the grammar is stated in two places that differ |
| `AC-2` | Every principal `ARCH-NATS-002` requires appears in the matrix with both its publish and its subscribe entries, or an explicit "none" | A principal is missing, or has a direction left unstated |
| `AC-3` | No permission wildcards across agent identifiers; every trailing wildcard is justified in its own row | A permission spans agent identifiers, or a wildcard carries no justification |
| `AC-4` | **All five of `ARCH-TEST-002`'s clauses appear as named negative cases** — an agent cannot publish commands, cannot consume another agent's commands, cannot publish another agent's results, cannot use revoked bootstrap credentials, cannot reach broker administration subjects | Any of the five has no corresponding case |
| `AC-5` | **Every subject role named in `ADR-0002` § 4 and `ADR-0003` § 3 has a grammar expression** | A role the prior ADRs named by role has no expression, and no finding records why it is unrepresentable |
| `AC-6` | One exact `FilterSubject` per consumer; any multi-filter use states its authorization behaviour explicitly | A consumer has more than one filter with no authorization account given |
| `AC-7` | Every authorization case names the principal, the subject, the direction, and the expected outcome | A case omits any of the four |
| `AC-8` | **No case is stated as executable.** No test path, no command line, no fixture claimed as produced | The ADR claims to run, generate or sign anything |

`AC-5` is the case that closes the chain. P02 and P03 wrote "named by role, the
grammar is P04's" in a dozen places, and until now nothing checked that P04
discharges those deferrals. **A role left unrepresentable is how an ADR chain
quietly fails to close** — each document correct on its own, and the obligation
lost between them.

`AC-8` is `G12`'s lesson made checkable rather than trusted. That task took four
review rounds because a contradiction between what a task was told to produce
and what could exist survived in five documents; a case is cheaper than a fifth
round.

### 5.3 Demonstrated failing, and what these cases cannot detect

Every case is demonstrated failing before acceptance, with the `DL-7`
assertions applied to each planting: the anchor is unique, the document changed,
the mutated document **actually carries the defect, verified by parsing it
directly**, and the reported failure names the case. P03's evidence records two
plantings that satisfied the first two assertions and not the third; the
predicates there asserted that text had changed rather than that the defect was
present, and P04's must not repeat it.

| Case | Cannot detect | Found instead by |
|---|---|---|
| `AC-1` | Whether the grammar is **well designed** — whether its token structure survives contact with a fleet, a rename, or a second deployment | Review; C03, where an awkward grammar becomes awkward configuration |
| `AC-2` | Whether a principal's permissions are the **right** ones. It checks presence and direction, not authority | Review; C03's negative identity matrix |
| `AC-3` | Whether a justified trailing wildcard is bounded as tightly as the entry allows | C03, where an excessive permission fails a denial test |
| `AC-4` | Whether a named negative case is **testable as written**, or whether it describes a refusal no configuration can produce | C03, when it cannot implement the case |
| `AC-5` | Whether the grammar expression for a role **means** what the prior ADR meant by that role | Review against `ADR-0002` § 4 and `ADR-0003` § 3 |
| `AC-6` | Whether the authorization account given for a multi-filter consumer is **correct** | Review; C03 |
| `AC-7` | Whether the expected outcome is the **right** outcome | Review; C14's adversarial suite |
| `AC-8` | A claim that is executable in substance while avoiding the vocabulary — "the matrix below is validated" states no path and still overclaims | Review |

`AC-4` and `AC-5` carry the most weight. Between them they assert that every
mandated refusal is named and every deferred role is expressed — and neither can
tell you a case is implementable or an expression faithful.

## 8. Security reviewer

**Codex, named here rather than at approval.**

Program rule 6 separates security-sensitive work from its acceptance review.
P04 decides the authorization boundary itself — the third of RFC 0001's six
cumulative security layers, and the one every other layer is measured against.
A permission granted too widely here is granted too widely everywhere.

The remit is the semantic properties in § 5.3: whether a permission grants the
right authority, whether a named refusal is producible, and whether each grammar
expression is faithful to the role the prior ADR deferred.

`DL-8` applies to how P04 acts on every finding. **Follow the steps in the
ledger entry itself, not a summary here.** `G12` is the cautionary case: four
review rounds, because each fix went to the instances reported and the sweep's
pattern covered one of the shape's several vocabularies.
