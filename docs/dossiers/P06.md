# Dossier — P06, Delivery and job lifecycle ADR

The task that decides what the system believes about a job, when it is entitled
to believe it, and what it says when it cannot know. The four ADRs before it
decided who may connect, who may say what, and what is inside a message. P06 is
the first that decides **what happens over time**, and the first whose subject
is failure rather than structure.

This dossier is a boundary, not an approval. Landing it authorizes nothing: P06
still needs its own presented plan and explicit approval. P06 is a planning and
ADR task and may use one pull request; it does not split into `-A` and `-I`.
Program rule 6's separation of implementation from acceptance review is met by
the independent reviewer named in § 8.

## 3. Exact outputs

One ADR, `docs/adr/0006-delivery-and-job-lifecycle.md`, containing:

1. **The server state machine** — every state, every transition, its trigger, and which transitions are terminal
2. **The agent state machine** — the same, and where the two machines are permitted to disagree
3. **Durable boundaries** — what must be on disk before what happens, stated as ordering obligations rather than as storage design (`ARCH-JOB-002`)
4. **Stream and consumer configuration** — expressed against `ADR-0002` § 7 and § 9, naming what P06 fixes and what P02 already fixed
5. **Acknowledgement and progress** — when an ack is sent, what `in-progress` signalling is permitted, and why neither weakens `ARCH-JOB-005`
6. **Publication deduplication** — `Nats-Msg-Id`, its window, and what it does *not* guarantee
7. **Application-ledger deduplication** — the agent ledger as the authority beyond any broker window (`ARCH-JOB-003`)
8. **Redelivery** — finite `MaxDeliver`, explicit `BackOff`, terminal advisories, and what each redelivery is permitted to cause (`ARCH-NATS-010`)
9. **Cancellation races** — cancellation arriving before, during and after execution, and at the exact boundary where a crash makes the outcome indeterminate (`THR-25`)
10. **Restart** — server restart and agent restart, separately, each with what is recovered and what cannot be
11. **Delivery failure and expiry** — when a job stops being deliverable, what it becomes, and who is told
12. **`UNKNOWN`** — when it is reported, what it is not permitted to be rendered as, and **how a verified late result reconciles with it while both observations remain** (`ARCH-JOB-004`)
13. **A lifecycle table** — every state by durability point, who may cause it, what the operator sees, and whether it is terminal

Enumerable content is carried as **tables**, so the acceptance cases read
structure rather than prose. States and transitions are enumerated
exhaustively; a state machine described only in prose cannot be checked for a
state with no way out.

## 11. Decisions requiring confirmation at P06 approval

### `D-P06-1` — `RSK-9` renews; it does not resolve

`RSK-9` names P06 as its expiry and is the only risk that does. **The expectation
recorded here is that P06 renews it**, and the reasoning is stated now so that a
P06 agent does not spend the task discovering it or, worse, resolve it because
the expiry said the task was due.

P06 makes the agent ledger authoritative (`ARCH-JOB-003`) and requires durable
receipt before execution (`ARCH-JOB-002`). Both strengthen what a **correct**
agent can prove. `RSK-9` is about an agent that is not correct: a compromised
host owns that ledger and signs results with its own key, so every mechanism P06
adds is a mechanism the attacker controls. The server gains no independent
witness, and attestation — which would be one — is outside Generation 2's narrow
promise and catalogued as Future (`CAP-IDENT-*`).

So the renewal is not a failure to make progress. It is the accurate statement
that this risk is not in P06's power to close, and the compensating control —
bounded to one host by construction — is unchanged.

**If the maintainer reads `RSK-9` as resolvable at P06, that changes the ADR's
scope materially** and is a decision to take at approval rather than in review.

### `D-P06-2` — the `ARCH-JOB-005` / `ARCH-NATS-010` interaction is P06's to answer

`ARCH-JOB-005` permits an acknowledgement only after receipt, terminal state and
result are durable **and the result publication receives a broker
acknowledgement**. `ARCH-NATS-010` requires finite `MaxDeliver`.

A job whose result publication keeps failing therefore exhausts its redelivery
budget without ever being acknowledged. Both invariants are satisfied and the
job is in a state neither describes. What the agent believes, what the server
believes, whether the terminal advisory is the server's signal, and whether this
is `UNKNOWN` or a distinct delivery failure are exactly P06's subject.

It is named here because no prior task had to reconcile the two, and a P06 that
restates both invariants without joining them would pass a reading that only
checked citations. `AC-7` is the case; this is why it exists.

### `D-P06-3` — a defect in `ADR-0002`, `0003`, `0004` or `0005` is raised, not fixed

P06 is the first task depending on four accepted ADRs at once, and § 3 item 4
requires it to express stream and consumer configuration against `ADR-0002` § 7
— the section most likely to be found wanting.

The rule does not bend for that. A defect is raised as a finding and the ADR
records it as raised, which is what `ADR-0005` did for the service public halves
and what produced `G15`. Fixing it in place would put P06's approval in front of
a change to an accepted document that its own reviewer never saw.

### `D-P06-4` — P06 specifies the lifecycle, it does not implement it

No code, no schema, no ledger. The durable boundaries are stated as ordering
obligations — *this is on disk before that happens* — and not as tables,
columns or file formats, which are C02's. `G12` and `G13` are why this is
written down: both existed because a P-stage task was asked for artifacts no
build existed to produce.
