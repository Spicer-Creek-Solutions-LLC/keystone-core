# Dossier — C03, NATS operator, account, identity and permission generation

The third Stage C task, and the first whose evidence needs a broker running.

C01 built the envelope and C02 built the stores. Neither crosses a wire. C03
generates the **NATS authorization configuration a deployment actually runs** —
the operator and account JWTs, the service-role users, the per-agent permission
template, and the account limits — and proves at a live broker that the
permissions grant exactly what `ADR-0004` § 4 says and refuse everything else.

**It is an identity and authorization task with no journey.** No command is
published, no result is consumed, no agent enrolls. `ADR-0010` § 4's harness
runs a broker so the generated JWTs can be exercised against it; nothing in C03
sends a Keystone message through one.

`test/e2e/docker/compose.yaml` was written expecting this task, and says so:

> C05 is the first task with a journey to run across this, and **C03 generates
> the production NATS configuration the broker will use** — `ADR-0010` § 3
> forbids a test-only one, so there is no placeholder here pretending to be it.

This dossier is a boundary, not an approval. Landing it authorizes nothing: C03
still needs its own presented plan and explicit approval, and it is a
**behaviour workstream**, so it splits into `C03-A` and `C03-I` with different
agents per [`REBOOT-EXECUTION-PLAN.md`](../project/REBOOT-EXECUTION-PLAN.md)
§ "Required task dossier". Roles alternate from C02: **`C03-A` is Codex's to
write and Claude's to review**, and `C03-I` the reverse.

### What C03 decides

**How the JWTs are built and where the generator's seams are.** It decides no
subject, no permission, no principal and no limit — those are `ADR-0002`'s and
`ADR-0004`'s. A C03 that finds a permission inconvenient and widens it has

**`ADR-0004` § 7 is explicit about this**: *"Every JWT's permission lists are
generated from § 4 and never hand-edited. A permission that exists in a deployed
JWT and not in § 4 is a defect in generation."*

## 5. Acceptance cases

### 5.1 The `TESTING.md` feature table

**Authorization denial is the row C03 owns** — *"an unauthorized NATS identity is
rejected by NATS"* — and C03 is the task that makes it mean something, because
`ADR-0010` § 3 forbids proving it against anything but generated production
JWTs.

Intended target, non-target, server isolation, payload protection, duplicate
delivery, restart, cancellation, audit and diagnostics are all `N/A`: C03 runs
no journey. § NATS security suite's payload-encryption and signature-validation
bullets are **C08's**, not C03's, though they execute in the same suite.

### 5.2 The contract's cases

**The case set is `ADR-0004`'s, taken by its own identifiers.** §§ 8 and 9 are
already a contract: twenty-three positive rows and fifteen negative rows, each a
principal, a subject, a direction and an outcome. `D-C03-3` says why they are
not renumbered.

`C03-A` adds a third family for the generator's own output, which §§ 8–9 do not
cover because they are about a running broker:

| Case | Fails when |
|---|---|
| `GEN-1` | Fewer or more than two accounts exist, or a Keystone principal holds credentials in the system account |
| `GEN-2` | Any principal of `ADR-0002` § 3 is missing, or two share an identity |
| `GEN-3` | A generated JWT carries a permission `ADR-0004` § 4 does not grant it |
| `GEN-4` | A permission `ADR-0004` § 4 grants is absent from the generated JWT |
| `GEN-5` | An account limit of `ADR-0002` § 9 is absent or infinite |
| `GEN-6` | The generator accepts an identifier the grammar refuses, or one reserved for a service principal |
| `GEN-7` | The operator seed is reachable from any process the deployment runs |

**`GEN-3` and `GEN-4` are the pair that matters**, and the reason is
`ADR-0004` § 8's own: *"A positive list shorter than the permission list lets C03
generate a JWT missing a granted permission and still pass every case."* One
direction catches a permission too wide, the other a permission too narrow, and
a suite with only the first is a suite that cannot see an agent quietly losing
the ability to publish its own results.

### 5.3 Demonstrated failing, and what these cases cannot detect

Every case is demonstrated against a planted defect before it is trusted, per
`DL-1`. Three traps specific to this task, each the *check cannot fail on an
input nobody looks at* shape that C01 produced four times and C02 twice:

- **A `$JS.API` case that cannot tell a permissions denial from a missing
  consumer.** `POS-7`, `POS-10`, `POS-14`, `POS-22` and `NEG-11`–`NEG-13` name
  JetStream API subjects. If the stream or consumer does not exist, an
  *authorized* publish fails too — with a different error. A case that asserts
  only "this failed" proves nothing about authorization. **Each must distinguish
  the broker's permissions violation from every other refusal.**
- **`NEG-7` is not a publish denial.** `ADR-0004` § 9 marks it: a revoked
  bootstrap identity **fails at connection**, because revocation removes the user
  from the account. *"A test that connects successfully and is refused on publish
  has proved something weaker than revocation."*
- **A generated-fixture suite that regenerates what it asserts.** `ADR-0010` § 3
  forbids committed JWTs, which removes the pinning C01 and C02 leaned on. The
  frozen thing must therefore be the **matrix**, not the artifact — and a case
  that compares a generated JWT against a permission list derived from the same
  code compares nothing. That is `AC-10`'s lesson from C02, arriving without the
  checked-in artifact that solved it there. `D-C03-3`.

**What these cases cannot detect.** That `ADR-0004` § 4's matrix is the *right*
matrix; that a permission is safe rather than merely as specified; that the
broker enforces what its configuration says on a version nobody has run
(`D-C03-5`).

## 11. Decisions requiring confirmation at C03 approval

### `D-C03-1` — how a NATS JWT gets signed, and the module's second dependency

`ADR-0002` § 2 requires decentralized operator/account/user JWTs. Nothing in any
ADR names a library.

**The module has exactly one dependency today**, added at C02 under `D-C02-1`
with a measured cost, so this is not a routine addition.

| Option | Evidence to gather before approval |
|---|---|
| **`github.com/nats-io/jwt/v2` + `github.com/nats-io/nkeys`** | The upstream libraries the broker itself validates with. Module and binary cost to be measured the way `D-C02-1` measured `modernc.org/sqlite`; both are pure Go, so `CGO_ENABLED=0` is not a constraint |
| The `nsc` CLI | A **binary** dependency in the image and in every developer's path, and configuration generated by shelling out. `ADR-0010` § 3 wants the deployment's code path, not a tool's |
| Hand-rolled | NATS JWTs are Ed25519-signed claims in a defined shape. Writing that by hand is re-implementing a specification whose validator is the broker; a divergence appears as an authorization failure with no diagnosis |

**Proposed: the upstream libraries.** The alternative that is not a
re-implementation is a CLI, and a CLI is the thing `ADR-0010` § 3 is written
against. What is worth confirming rather than assuming is **the cost and the
trust**: two modules whose transitive graph has not been measured, entering a
tree that has had ten, and a library that mints credentials rather than reading
a file.

### `D-C03-2` — where an acceptance contract lives when it needs a broker

C01's and C02's contracts are `go test -tags contract ./test/contract/<pkg>` and
need nothing but a filesystem. **C03's needs a broker to answer it**, and
`REQUIREMENTS-TRACEABILITY.md` names its evidence in `test/e2e/docker/`.

Three ways, and they are not equivalent:

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Frozen surface in `test/contract/authorization/`, cases drive a broker container** | The contract machinery works unchanged — `contract`, `pending-contract` and `contract-immutability-check` all enumerate `test/contract/*/`. **Four register rows change directory**, which is a deliberate edit to an approved document |
| Frozen surface inside `test/e2e/docker/` | The register is untouched, but a frozen surface would sit in a package that already holds P11b's unfrozen tests, and three gates' globs would have to learn a second location |
| Two places — surface in one, execution in the other | A mapping to maintain between them, which is the second copy this repository keeps removing |

**Proposed: the first.** The register's evidence column names files whose
directory is an implementation detail of where the suite lives; the frozen
surface's location is not. But moving four approved rows is the maintainer's
call, not a reviewer's.

### `D-C03-3` — what is frozen when fixtures are forbidden

`ADR-0010` § 3: *"Fixtures are generated, never committed. A committed JWT is a
JWT that stops matching the generator."*

C01 froze hand-written framing vectors and C02 froze a checked-in schema
artifact, and in both cases the pinned artifact is what stopped a **consistent
retune** — a change to the code and its expectations together — passing every
gate. C03 may not pin its artifact.

**Proposed: freeze the matrix, by its own identifiers.** `C03-A`'s surface
carries `POS-1`–`POS-23`, `NEG-1`–`NEG-15` and `GEN-1`–`GEN-7` with the
requirement text `ADR-0004` §§ 8–9 already state. The generated JWTs are inputs,
never expectations.

**Not renumbered into `AC-*`.** `ADR-0004`'s identifiers are already the names
these cases have in an accepted document, and renumbering creates a mapping
between two documents that must agree — the shape `G39` and `G41` each had to
repair. `pending-contract` scopes identifiers per manifest and requires no
prefix; the protocol package already registers `nightly-fuzz-search`.

**Open, and the reason this is a decision rather than a note:** without a pinned
artifact, what catches a generator and a test that drift together? The candidate
is a published permission set an operator reads — `docs/project/NATS-AUTHORIZATION.md`,
the shape `D-C02-2` gave the ledger schema — compared against both. That costs a
document and a gate, and it may be the only thing standing between this suite
and a consistent retune.

### `D-C03-4` — the line between C03 and C05, written out

`ADR-0004` § 7's table of *"what C03 generates"* includes the **bootstrap user
JWT** and the **account revocation list**, while `ARCH-NATS-004`'s register row
lands at **C05**. Both are correct and the seam has to be explicit, because
`C02-A` froze a case whose property belonged to another task and no
implementation could satisfy it.

| | C03 | C05 |
|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap identity | Generates the JWT **shape**: two `ks.enroll.<token>.*` entries, expiring with the token | Issues one per token, runs `ADR-0003` § 6's six stages, recovers per § 7 |
| Revocation | Generates an account whose revocation list can be written, and `NEG-7` proves a revoked identity cannot connect | Performs the revocation at S5 and verifies it at S6 |
| Agent identity | Generates the **template** with `<its own id>` substituted | Mints the real one against the agent's recorded public key |
| The `<id>` itself | **Validates** it against `internal/protocol.ValidIdentifier` and `ReservedSender`, and refuses a non-conforming one | Neither task assigns it — see below |

**`ADR-0004` § 1's open finding now has a second dependent.** The grammar
requires `<id>` to be *server-assigned, never operator-supplied*; `ADR-0003`
records only the operator's `--agent-name` label, which the charter defines as a
label and not a claim. `D-P04-5` required this raised and stopped at, and it has
not been resolved.

**C03 is not blocked by it**, because the generator consumes an identifier and
validates it; where it came from is its caller's business. **C03 must not
resolve it either** — the origin is `ADR-0003`'s, and settling it under a C03
approval would amend an accepted ADR sideways. It is recorded here so that C05
does not discover it as a blocker the way `C01-I` discovered `ADR-0005`'s.

**A second seam: `ARCH-NATS-007`'s limits.** `ADR-0002` § 9 says *"C03 must
render into configuration"*, and its table mixes account-scoped limits with
`KS_CMD`, `KS_RES` and per-consumer limits. The streams and consumers are C06's
to create. **Proposed: C03 renders every account-scoped limit and the stream and
consumer limit *values*; C06 applies the latter when it creates them.** If the
maintainer prefers the whole table at C06, § 4's `ARCH-NATS-007` row changes
accordingly.

### `D-C03-5` — the broker version this repository measures authorization by

`compose.yaml` pins `nats:2-alpine` — a floating minor. For P11b, whose tests
assert network isolation and that a container starts, that is harmless. **For
C03 it is the enforcement engine**: every positive and negative case in §§ 8–9
is a statement about what a particular broker does with a particular JWT.

A floating tag means a broker upgrade can turn a permission result red with no
change in this repository, and — worse — can turn one **green** that should not
be.

**Proposed: pin an exact version**, and record the upgrade as a deliberate
change the way `feedback_versioning_no_minor_pins` requires of everything else.
The cost is that upgrades stop being automatic, which for this dependency is the
point.

## 12. Scope this task must not absorb

- **The enrollment protocol** — C05. C03 generates bootstrap and agent shapes;
  running the six stages, recovering from a crash in any of them, and revoking
  at S5 are C05's. `D-C03-4` draws the line.
- **Creating streams and consumers** — C06. C03 may create a stream or consumer
  as a **generated fixture** so the `$JS.API` cases can distinguish a
  permissions denial from a missing target; the production stream configuration,
  its retention and its consumer policy are `ADR-0002` § 7's and C06's.
- **Publishing or consuming a Keystone message** — C06 and C08. Nothing here
  puts an envelope on a subject; the cases assert authorization outcomes, not
  message delivery.
- **Payload encryption and signature validation at the broker boundary** — C08.
  `TESTING.md` § NATS security suite lists them in the same suite; they are not
  the same task.
- **Accounts, users and directories on the host** — C13. C03 generates JWTs; it
  does not create the operating-system accounts that hold them, and no case here
  may assert a property of something C03 does not create.
- **Resolving the agent identifier's origin** — `ADR-0003`'s, unowned. Raised in
  `D-C03-4`, not fixed here.
