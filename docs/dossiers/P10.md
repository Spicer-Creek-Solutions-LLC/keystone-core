# Dossier — P10, Acceptance-harness design

The task that decides what would count as proof. Every ADR in Stage P states
properties a document can assert and only a running system can hold, and each
one named the harness as where its claim stops being a claim. P10 designs that
harness.

**It does not build it.** No Go module, build or test runner exists until P11,
and P11 follows P00–P10. That constraint shapes this dossier more than any
other: P10 specifies evidence, and every task that could *produce* evidence is
downstream of it. `D-P10-2` is about what that means for three risks whose
renewals said otherwise.

This dossier is a boundary, not an approval. Landing it authorizes nothing: P10
still needs its own presented plan and explicit approval. P10 is a planning task
and may use one pull request; it does not split into `-A` and `-I`. Program rule
6's separation of implementation from acceptance review is met by the
independent reviewer named in § 8.

## 3. Exact outputs

**What artifact P10 produces is `D-P10-4` and is not settled here.** The list
below is what P10 must decide, whichever container it lands in:

1. **The isolated Docker topology** — concrete services, networks and the proof
   that server-to-agent and agent-to-agent direct connection fails, which
   `TESTING.md` § "Required test topology" requires as a *network probe* rather
   than an assumption.
2. **Production NATS configuration generation** — how the harness produces the
   account, JWTs and permissions `ADR-0002` and `ADR-0004` specify, **without a
   test-only authorization adapter** (`ARCH-TEST-002`).
3. **Binary packaging for the harness** — which artifacts the topology runs, and
   how that stays the packaged path (`ARCH-COMM-003`).
4. **Fault controls** — the mechanisms by which the harness *causes* each fault
   the ADRs describe: broker loss, redelivery exhaustion, crash between the
   receipt and the start, a late result after reconciliation, a corrupt ledger.
5. **External-effect probes** — how a test proves an effect happened on the
   intended agent and **did not** happen on the server or a non-target
   (`ARCH-TEST-001`).
6. **Negative identities** — the adversarial identity matrix, derived from
   `ADR-0004`'s negative list and `ADR-0009` § 2's refused principal.
7. **Artifact retention and the VM boundary** — what is kept, for how long, and
   which cases cannot run in Docker at all.
8. **The scale profile** — what `TESTING.md` § Nightly's hundred agents for an
   hour actually measures, and what a failure of it means.
9. **Local and CI entry points** — one command locally, and the gate schedule's
   division between pull request, merge, nightly and release candidate.
10. **Artifact classification, redaction and canary seeding** — private-key,
    token, command and output canaries, scanned before upload. **Subject to
    `D-P10-3`.**
11. **The invariant-to-evidence map** — all 24, and its relationship to
    `REQUIREMENTS-TRACEABILITY.md`. **Subject to `D-P10-4`.**
12. **Disposition of the six residual risks** that expire here. **Subject to
    `D-P10-1` and `D-P10-2`.**
13. **Deferred decision points**, each with what would trigger it.

## 11. Decisions requiring confirmation at P10 approval

### `D-P10-1` — six risks expire here, and four of them P10 cannot move

`RSK-1`, `RSK-4`, `RSK-9`, `RSK-12`, `RSK-13` and `RSK-14` all name P10. That is
double P08's three and more than any task in the epic. It was derived by reading
the expiry cell of every row in `THREAT-MODEL.md` § 9, not from the epic's
summary of them.

**Three belong here.** `RSK-1`, `RSK-4` and `RSK-9` were sent to P10 by P08 for a
stated reason — P10 is where a compensating control that has only ever been
asserted could be given a demonstration. `RSK-1`'s separation of keys is
`ARCH-TEST-002`'s negative-identity matrix; `RSK-4`'s forensic reconstruction
from agent ledgers is a probe the harness could specify; `RSK-9` is bounded by
construction and the harness can at least exercise the boundary.

**Three are key custody, and a harness design does not touch them.** `RSK-12` is
the absence of rotation and revocation for the service keys. `RSK-13` is the
absence of a rotation *procedure*. `RSK-14` is the tampered token bundle, whose
real mitigation `ADR-0005` § 14 calls *a channel-separated deployment mode*.
None of those is created by specifying Docker topology and fault injection.

**The question is whether they were gated correctly.** `C13` owns packaging,
install and key material; `C14` owns adversarial closure. A risk pointed at P10
because P10 was the next unclaimed milestone renews here for no reason and
expires again at a task that also cannot move it.

The options, and the dossier takes none of them without a decision:

- **Renew all six at P10.** Honest about P10's reach and leaves the gates wrong.
- **Renew three, re-gate three** to `C13` or `C14` with the reason recorded.
  Changes a gate P05 and P03 set, which is a threat-model edit and needs
  arguing at approval, not performing.
- **Resolve any where planned evidence is enough.** Probably none: planned
  evidence is not evidence, which is `D-P10-2`.

### `D-P10-2` — P10 cannot demonstrate anything, and three renewals said it could

**That is not achievable as written, and the error is P08's.** No Go module,

**So P10 inherits three risks expecting a demonstration it cannot make.** The

**This is raised as a defect in an accepted ADR, not repaired by P10.**
`ADR-0008` is out of bounds in § 2, and a P10 that edited a prior ADR to make its
own obligation achievable would be doing what `D-P07-1` exists to prevent. If

### `D-P10-3` — `D-P09-5`'s contradiction lands here, and here it can be settled

`TESTING.md` § "Required test topology" says **"P09 defines artifact
classification and redaction"**. `REBOOT-EXECUTION-PLAN.md` § P10 says **"Define
artifact classification and redaction. Seed private-key, token, command, and

P09 raised it and refused to fix it: `TESTING.md` was out of its bounds, and a
task that edits a normative document to delete its own inconvenient obligation
is the shape `D-P07-1` exists to prevent. **P09 was right to refuse and P10 is
differently placed**, because the plan assigns the work to P10 — so P10
correcting `TESTING.md` is a task taking on an obligation rather than shedding
one.

- **P10 takes it**, and `TESTING.md`'s sentence is corrected in P10's own pull
  request as a consequence of P10 doing the work. `TESTING.md` would be added to
  **P10's** allowed paths in § 3, not this dossier's.
- **A `G` task corrects it first**, and P10 depends on that commit.

The first is fewer pull requests and puts the correction next to the work that
justifies it. The second keeps a normative document's correction separate from a
task that benefits from it, which is the more conservative reading of the same
principle P09 invoked.

### `D-P10-4` — what P10 produces, and whether its invariant map is a second register

**Two questions that have to be answered together.**

`REQUIREMENTS-TRACEABILITY.md` already maps **all 24 invariants** to planned
evidence, with a design owner and a gate per row. The plan tells P10 to *"map
every architecture invariant to planned evidence"*. Those are the same sentence.

And the register names **"Test architecture ADR"** as the design owner for
`ARCH-COMM-003`, `ARCH-TEST-001` and others. **No such ADR exists.** The register
is pointing at a document that P10 is evidently supposed to produce.

So:

- **`ADR-0010`, a test-architecture ADR**, which is what the register already
  calls it. P10's map then **updates the register's rows** rather than creating a
  second table — one source of truth, and the register's dangling owner is
  resolved.
- **A design document** such as `docs/project/ACCEPTANCE-HARNESS.md`, on the
  grounds that a harness is not an architecture decision. The register's "Test
  architecture ADR" would then be corrected to name it.

**Either way the map must not be duplicated.** Two enumerations of one mapping is
this project's most-recurring defect, and `AC-8` requires the relationship to be
stated rather than left for a reader to infer.

I lean toward **`ADR-0010`**: the register named it, the decisions in § 3 are
architectural (production paths only, no test-only adapter, what the VM boundary
is), and every prior Stage P task from P02 produced an ADR.
